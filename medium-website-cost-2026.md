## What Makes the Price Go Up

Five factors that actually matter:

**1. Number of pages.** A landing page is one page. A corporate site can have 15–30 pages. Each page takes 2–4 hours to build.

**2. Design complexity.** Template-based design: cheap. Custom UI/UX with animations, custom illustrations, brand identity: more expensive.

**3. Functionality.** A contact form is standard. A calculator, personal dashboard, CRM integration, payment processing — each adds $200–500.

**4. Content.** If you have text and photos, great. If not, someone needs to write copy, take photos, create graphics. That's $50–200 per page.

**5. SEO promotion.** Basic SEO is included. Ongoing monthly promotion is separate: $70–300/month.

## The Costs Nobody Mentions

This is where studios get you. They quote $600 for the website, but forget to mention:

- **Hosting renewal**: $5–20/month (after the first free year)
- **Domain renewal**: $10–15/year
- **SSL certificate**: free (Let's Encrypt) or $50+/year for premium
- **Maintenance**: $50–150/month for updates and fixes
- **Email hosting**: $3–10/month

That "cheap" $600 website actually costs $800–1,200/year to keep running.

We include the first year of everything in our price. After that, maintenance starts at $50/month. No hidden fees.

## Freelancer vs Studio vs Website Builder

I've worked as all three. Here's the honest comparison:

| | Freelancer | Studio | Builder (Wix/Squarespace) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Landing page** | $100–300 | $250–600 | $0–30/mo |
| **Design quality** | Varies wildly | Consistent | Templates |
| **SEO** | Basic | Full | Limited |
| **Support** | Often vanishes | Guaranteed | DIY |
| **You own the site** | Yes | Yes | No (rental) |
| **Long-term cost** | Low | Medium | High (monthly forever) |

The builder trap: Wix looks free until you realize you're paying $30/month forever and don't own anything. After 2 years, you've paid $720 for a template site you can't move.

A freelancer is cheaper but riskier. Some are great, some disappear after taking your money.

A studio costs more upfront but gives you a working site you own, with support if something breaks.

## How to Not Overpay

Four things that actually save money:

1. **Prepare your content first.** Have text, photos, and a logo ready before development starts. Every round of revisions adds time and cost.

2. **Start small.** A landing page for $250 covers 90% of what most businesses need. You can always add pages later.

3. **Don't pay for features you won't use.** You don't need a blog, a calculator, and a CRM on day one. Start with the essentials.

4. **Ask for a fixed price.** Hourly billing is a black box. Fixed price means you know exactly what you're paying.

Don't overpay for enterprise features you'll never use.

Pay once, get a working website, and spend your money on getting customers.
