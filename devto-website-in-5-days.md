# A Website in 5 Days: What Actually Fits in the Timeline (2026)

"How fast can you build it?" is the question every founder asks in the first call. The honest answer is: **a landing page in 3 days, a business website in 5–7, an online store in 3–4 weeks** — and anything else is a sales pitch.

Here is what a 5-day website actually looks like when it works, what it costs, and what we refuse to promise.

## What fits into 5 days (and what does not)

| Scope | Timeline | Realistic? | Price range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landing page for an ad campaign | 3–5 days | Yes | $250–600 |
| Business card site (up to 7 pages) | 5–7 days | Yes | $400–1,200 |
| Corporate site (10–20 pages) | 10–15 days | No | $900–2,500 |
| Online store (up to 2,000 products) | 15–30 days | No | $1,500–4,000 |
| Custom web app / SaaS dashboard | 4–8 weeks | No | $3,000+ |

The rule of thumb: **5 days × 1 competent developer = roughly 40–50 hours of work.** Everything that fits in that budget fits in 5 days. Everything else needs a plan, not a deadline.

## A day-by-day plan that actually works

**Day 1 — brief, structure, and copy.** We map the pages, agree on what each page must make the visitor do, and collect the text. If the copy is not ready by the end of day 1, the deadline moves — this is the single most common reason a 5-day project becomes a 3-week project.

**Day 2 — design.** One visual direction, adapted to three breakpoints. We design the home page first, because if the home page works, the internal pages follow the same system.

**Day 3 — layout and forms.** All pages get built and the forms, buttons, and calls to action are wired up. Most of the "content" of a website is structure, not text.

**Day 4 — content, integrations, and analytics.** Text goes in, images are compressed, the form starts sending leads into your CRM or Telegram, analytics and pixel are installed.

**Day 5 — testing and launch.** Cross-browser check, mobile check, speed check, form test, domain and SSL, publish, hand over the access and a short guide.

No step allows scope to grow. Anything discovered in day 3 that is not in the brief becomes a separate estimate.

## What makes it possible

- **Fixed scope.** One page, one goal, one call to action. A 5-day site is not a 5-day website for every conceivable purpose.
- **Content in advance.** Images and text prepared before day 1. We can write it for you, but it must be approved quickly.
- **A template that fits.** We use a proven structure when it fits the brief instead of designing from scratch. Custom design costs days.
- **One revision round per day.** Structured feedback, not an open comment thread.

## The price question nobody asks first

A 5-day landing page in 2026 costs $250–600. A business card site costs $400–1,200. Compare that with a "cheaper" option: a template you edit yourself, which is free but eats 10–20 hours of a specialist's time, plus the ongoing cost of a builder that limits SEO, speed, and integrations.

The real hidden costs of a rushed website are not the budget — they are the wrong structure, slow pages, and forms that send leads nowhere.

## The checklist before you commit to 5 days

- [ ] One clear goal for the site (a call, a purchase, a sign-up).
- [ ] Texts and images ready, or a budget for us to write them.
- [ ] Domain access (or permission to buy one).
- [ ] One decision-maker who can approve within 24 hours.
- [ ] A scope list written down, with everything you might want marked as "phase 2".
