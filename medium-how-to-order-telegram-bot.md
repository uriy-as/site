# How to Order a Telegram Bot: Step-by-Step Guide

A Telegram bot can handle customer support 24/7, collect leads, automate sales, and schedule appointments — all inside a messaging app your clients already use. But how do you order one without wasting money on a toy that nobody uses?

Here's a step-by-step guide based on real projects we've delivered.

## Step 1: Define the Problem

Before talking to any developer, answer one question: **what specific problem will this bot solve?**

Common use cases:

- **Customer support** — answer FAQs (pricing, hours, location) automatically
- **Lead collection** — capture inquiries and forward them to your CRM or Telegram
- **Sales automation** — generate quotes, process payments, send confirmations
- **Appointments** — coordinate scheduling without back-and-forth messages
- **Broadcasts** — send promotions, updates, and newsletters

Without a clear goal, the bot becomes a cool demo that never generates revenue.

## Step 2: Choose the Bot Type

| Type | What it does | Price range | Timeline |
|------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| **Basic bot** | Buttons, menus, canned answers | $130–200 | 3–5 days |
| **AI-powered bot** | Understands natural language, converses like a human | $250–400 | 5–10 days |
| **Integrated bot** | Connects to CRM, payment gateway, database | $400–800 | 2–4 weeks |

A basic bot is fine for a business card or FAQ. An AI bot is better for support and consulting — it handles 80% of routine questions without human intervention.

## Step 3: Write a Brief (Yes, Even a Short One)

You don't need a 50-page document. Just answer these questions:

1. **Goal** — one or two sentences (e.g., "Accept food delivery orders via Telegram")
2. **Audience** — who will use it (customers, employees, partners)
3. **Features** — what the bot should do (list 5–10 items)
4. **Branding** — logo, colors, tone of voice
5. **Integrations** — CRM, payment system, database, website
6. **Examples** — share 2–3 bots you like and explain why

This brief saves hours of back-and-forth and ensures you get what you actually need.

## Step 4: Find the Right Developer

**Where to look:**

- **Studio** — contract, warranty, post-launch support. More expensive but reliable.
- **Freelancer** — cheaper, but higher risk of disappearance after delivery.
- **Telegram channels** — many bot developers active in tech communities.

**What to check:**

- **Portfolio** — have they built similar bots?
- **Reviews** — what do past clients say?
- **Contract** — are they willing to sign one?
- **Support** — what happens after launch? Who fixes bugs?
- **Communication** — do they understand your requirements?

Red flag: someone promising a complex bot in "1 day" for "$50."

## Step 5: Agree on Price and Timeline

Real pricing for Telegram bots in 2026:

| Complexity | Price | Timeline |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| Basic (buttons + FAQ) | $130–200 | 3–5 days |
| AI-powered (GPT/Cohere) | $250–400 | 5–10 days |
| With integrations (CRM, payments) | $400–800 | 2–4 weeks |
| Enterprise (multi-step flows, admin panel) | $800–2000+ | 4–8 weeks |

**Important:** Ask what's included. Many developers quote only the build cost and forget about hosting ($5–15/month) and maintenance.

## Step 6: Test Before You Pay

Before accepting delivery, check:

1. **All scenarios** — walk through every conversation path
2. **Error handling** — type invalid data, send unexpected messages
3. **Speed** — does the bot respond within 1–2 seconds?
4. **Mobile** — is everything readable on a phone screen?
5. **Edge cases** — what happens if the server is down?

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **No clear goal** — "I just want a bot" leads to a bot that does nothing useful
- **Skipping the brief** — without requirements, the result is unpredictable
- **Choosing by price alone** — the cheapest option usually costs more when you rebuild it
- **No support plan** — bots need updates, bug fixes, and content changes
- **Ignoring hosting** — a bot needs a server to run 24/7 ($5–15/month)

## What's Next?

Once your bot is live:

1. **Monitor usage** — track how many conversations, what questions, where people drop off
2. **Iterate** — add features based on real user behavior
3. **Integrate** — connect to your website, CRM, or payment system
4. **Scale** — if the bot handles 60% of inquiries, consider adding AI capabilities

## Summary

Ordering a Telegram bot isn't rocket science — but it requires preparation. Define the problem, write a brief, choose a developer with a track record, and test thoroughly before paying.

A well-built bot pays for itself within the first month through saved support time and captured leads.
