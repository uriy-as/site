# Can You Really Build a Website in 5 Days? (Honest Answer)

"How fast can you build it?" is the question every founder asks in the first call. The honest answer: **a landing page in 3 days, a business website in 5-7, an online store in 3-4 weeks** — and anything else is a sales pitch.

Here is what a 5-day website actually looks like when it works, what it costs, and what we refuse to promise.

## What fits into 5 days (and what does not)

| Scope | Timeline | Realistic? | Price range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landing page for an ad campaign | 3-5 days | Yes | $250-600 |
| Business card site (up to 7 pages) | 5-7 days | Yes | $400-1,200 |
| Corporate site (10-20 pages) | 10-15 days | No | $900-2,500 |
| Online store (up to 2,000 products) | 15-30 days | No | $1,500-4,000 |
| Custom web app / SaaS dashboard | 4-8 weeks | No | $3,000+ |

The rule of thumb: **5 days × 1 competent developer = roughly 40-50 hours of work.** Everything that fits in that budget fits in 5 days. Everything else needs a plan, not a deadline.

## A day-by-day plan that actually works

**Day 1 — brief, structure, and copy.** We map the pages, agree on what each page must make the visitor do, and collect the text. If the copy is not ready by the end of day 1, the deadline moves — this is the single most common reason a 5-day project becomes a 3-week project.

**Day 2 — design.** Wireframes → visual design → client approval. One round of revisions included. If the client wants 5 rounds of revisions, add 3 days.

**Day 3 — development.** HTML/CSS, CMS setup, forms, analytics. The boring part that takes 60% of the time.

**Day 4 — content and SEO.** Text on pages, meta tags, images optimized, schema markup. This is where most "5-day" projects cut corners — and why they rank on page 5 instead of page 1.

**Day 5 — testing and launch.** Cross-browser, mobile, speed test, DNS switch, SSL. The site goes live.

## What makes a 5-day project fail

1. **No ready content.** The client says "I'll send the text tomorrow" and sends it on day 4. Deadline missed.
2. **Scope creep.** "Can we also add a blog? And a chatbot? And payment integration?" Each addition adds 2-3 days.
3. **No decision-maker available.** The designer needs approval, the client is on vacation, 2 days lost.
4. **Unrealistic expectations.** "I want it like Apple's website but in 5 days." Sure.

## What we refuse to promise

- We will not build an online store in 5 days. Period.
- We will not promise SEO results in 5 days. SEO takes 3-6 months.
- We will not skip testing to meet a deadline. A broken site launched on time is worse than a working site launched 2 days late.

## The price of speed

Speed costs money. A 5-day project costs 20-40% more than the same project in 10 days, because:
- Developer works overtime
- Design is compressed (less iteration)
- Testing is rushed (more bugs)

If your budget is tight, take 10 days. If your deadline is real (product launch, ad campaign), pay for speed.

## Bottom line

A 5-day website is real. It just has to be the right scope: landing page or small business site, ready content, one decision-maker, no scope creep. Anything bigger needs a realistic timeline.
