# Website Maintenance: The Cost Nobody Talks About (Until It's Too Late)

You paid $600 for your website. It looks great. It works perfectly. Then six months later, the plugins are outdated, the SSL certificate expires, and a hacker finds a vulnerability in your CMS.

Sound familiar?

Here's what website maintenance actually costs — and why skipping it is the most expensive decision you can make.

---

## Why Maintenance Matters

A website isn't a one-time purchase. It's a living system that needs regular care:

- **Software updates**: CMS, plugins, themes — all need patching
- **Security**: New vulnerabilities appear daily. Unpatched sites get hacked
- **Backups**: If something breaks, you need a recent backup to restore
- **Uptime monitoring**: If your site goes down at 3 AM, you need to know
- **Content updates**: New prices, new services, new photos

Skip maintenance for 6 months, and you're looking at a potential rebuild that costs more than the original development.

## What Happens Without Maintenance

I've seen it happen too many times:

**Month 1–3**: Nothing visible. Site works fine.
**Month 4–6**: Plugins show "update available" notifications. You ignore them.
**Month 7–9**: SSL certificate expires. Browser shows "Not Secure." Visitors leave.
**Month 10–12**: Hacker exploits an outdated plugin. Site goes down or gets defaced.
**Month 13+**: You're paying $500–1,000 for an emergency rebuild that should have cost $50/month.

The math is simple: $50/month × 12 = $600/year for maintenance. An emergency rebuild costs $500–1,000+ and takes weeks.

---

## How to Save on Maintenance

1. **Bundle edits.** Instead of emailing "change this price" every day, collect all changes into one monthly request. Most contractors charge less for batch work.

2. **Use a content calendar.** Plan your updates in advance. Know what's changing next month so you can prepare content early.

3. **Choose a long-term contract.** Monthly rates are higher. Quarterly or annual contracts usually come with 10–20% discounts.

4. **Learn basic edits.** If you can change a text paragraph or swap a photo in your CMS, you save billable hours for the complex stuff.

5. **Don't cheap out on hosting.** $3/month shared hosting means slow performance and security risks. $10–20/month VPS gives you speed and reliability.

## The Real Cost of "Free"

If your website was "free" (built by a friend, done yourself with a builder, or provided as part of a package), maintenance is still a cost:

- **Wix/Squarespace**: $16–45/month (you're renting, not owning)
- **WordPress on cheap hosting**: $3–5/month hosting + your time for updates
- **Friend-built site**: Whatever you pay them to fix things when they break

Nothing is truly free. The question is whether you pay upfront for quality or later for fixes.
